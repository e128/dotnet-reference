# Inclusive and Accessible Writing

Rules for inclusive language, accessibility, prescriptive wording, future features and third-party content.

## Inclusive Language

- Use gender-neutral wording: "person-hours", "humanity". Use singular "they".
- Avoid ableist words. Write "final check", not "sanity check". Write "slows down the service", not "cripples".
- Avoid violent or graphic verbs. Write "the connection doesn't respond", not "hangs". Write "click **New**", not "hit".
- Use non-divisive terms: "allowlist" and "denylist", "parent node". Do not pair "master" with "slave".
- Write "person with disabilities" and "uses a wheelchair". Avoid "the disabled", "normal" and "suffering from".
- Vary names, genders, ages and places in examples.
- Avoid metaphor and idiom. Use the precise term.

## Accessibility

- Use semantic HTML: `em` for emphasis, heading tags in order, `th` with `scope` for table headers.
- Write link text that makes sense out of context. Say when a link downloads a file or opens a new tab.
- Give every informative image alt text. Do not put new information only in an image, and do not use images of text or code.
- Do not rely on color, size or location alone. Do not use directional words such as "above" or "right-side".
- Keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or better. Make all functions work with a keyboard alone.
- Avoid all caps. Avoid "&" in place of "and".
- Give video and audio captions or a transcript.

## Prescriptive Wording

- Use "must" for a requirement. Do not use "should", because it is ambiguous.
- Use "we recommend" for advice and "can" for an option.
- Use "can" or "might" for a possible outcome: "The process can take 30 minutes".
- Show the common case first and move optional parameters to a reference.

## Future Features and Outside Sources

- Document present capability only. Do not announce an unreleased feature, directly or with "eventually".
- Paraphrase third-party content and link to the original. Do not copy it.
- When reuse rights are unclear, paraphrase and link.
