# Language and Grammar Rules

Rules for abbreviations, articles, capitalization, person, tense, pronouns, plurals and contractions.

## Abbreviations

- Use an abbreviation only when it saves the reader time. Spell out an unfamiliar term on first use and give the abbreviation in parentheses: "data manipulation language (DML)".
- Do not spell out common terms such as API, HTML, PDF, URL and USB.
- Write acronyms without periods (CIA, CA). Choose "a" or "an" by pronunciation: "a SQL", "an SAP".
- Do not use an acronym as a verb, and do not use internet slang such as "tl;dr" or "ymmv".

## Articles and Capitalization

- Keep articles (a, an, the) in every sentence, heading and title: "Create a VM instance".
- Use standard American capitalization. Do not use capital letters to carry meaning.
- Follow the official form of a product name, and match a UI label as shown.
- After a colon, use lowercase unless a proper noun, a quotation or a label such as Note follows.
- Avoid all caps and camel case in prose unless the text is an official name or code. Do not name a casing style: show an example such as `AssertionAccount`.
- Hyphenated words: capitalize only the first element unless a later one is a proper noun.

## Person, Pronouns and Anthropomorphism

- Address the reader as "you". Do not write "we", "our" or "us" for the reader, and do not call the reader "the user".
- "We" is fine for the organization when the antecedent is clear.
- Name the audience once, and do not shift it mid-document.
- Give every pronoun a clear antecedent. Follow "this" and "that" with a noun: "Set this value to true".
- Use singular "they" and no gendered pronouns.
- Use "that" for a restrictive clause, and "which" with a comma for a nonrestrictive clause.
- Do not give software human traits. Write "The PC detects a new device", not "sees".

## Tense and Mood

- Use present tense for general behavior: "The server sends an acknowledgment".
- Use future tense only for a real delayed action. Do not use hypothetical "would".
- Use imperative for instructions. In reference text, use third-person present verbs ("Gets the profile").

## Plurals and Contractions

- Use regular US plurals. Never use an apostrophe: "APIs", "OSes".
- Do not write optional plurals in parentheses, and do not pluralize a unit abbreviation ("64 GB").
- A compound subject joined by "and" takes a plural verb. After "one or more", use a plural.
- A number equal to 1 is singular. Zero, decimals and numbers above 1 are plural.
- Do not pluralize or make possessive a product or trademark name.
- Common contractions are fine ("you're", "don't", "can't"). Do not invent nonstandard ones.
