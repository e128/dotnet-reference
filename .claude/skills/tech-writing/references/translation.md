# Write for Translation

Use clear, consistent English so readers can understand and translate technical content.

Write for readers with different language backgrounds. Short, direct sentences also reduce translation effort and layout problems.

## Write for Global Readers

- Prefer familiar words and one clear meaning for each term.
- Use active voice, present tense, and subject, verb, object order.
- Keep sentences short. Put conditions before instructions, and keep modifiers near the words they describe.
- Limit noun strings to two modifiers. Rewrite longer strings to show the relationship.
- Use the same term for the same concept. Repeat a noun when a pronoun could be unclear.
- Explain abbreviations and technical terms. Name the type of code item, such as a YAML file.
- Use helper words such as that, then, and of when they clarify a relationship.
- Use unambiguous dates and times. Avoid local idioms, humor, slang, holidays, and seasonal references.
- Use varied names in examples. Put new information in text, not only in screenshots or images.
- Keep each word's meaning stable. Avoid switching a word between noun and verb use close together.
- Keep helper words and relative pronouns when they clarify which item or condition you mean.
- Use standard, parallel phrasing for repeated tasks and list items. Keep capitalization and punctuation consistent.
- Do not use directional references such as above or below in procedures.
- Use plurals and possessives by the regular rules. Contractions are allowed, and common ones such as "don't" are fine.

## Related Terms

- **Localization**: Adapt a product and its content to a place, including local formats and conventions.
- **Translation**: Convert content from one language to another. The work can include localization.
- **Internationalization**: Design a product or document so later localization requires less change.

Source: [Google guidance on translation](https://developers.google.com/style/translation).
