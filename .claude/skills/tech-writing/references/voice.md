# Use Active Voice

Use active voice to show who performs an action, and use passive voice when it serves the reader.

Active voice puts the actor in the subject position. It helps readers see who acts and who owns a task.

## Prefer Active Voice

Write “You send a request, and the server returns confirmation.” Avoid “A request is sent, and confirmation is returned” when the actor matters.

Do not force active voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or less important than the result.

## Use Passive Voice When It Helps

- Focus on an object when its state matters more than its actor, such as “The file is saved.”
- Do not blame the reader when the actor does not affect the useful fact.
- State an outcome without naming an irrelevant actor, such as when a database was cleared.
- Give enough context for readers to know whether they must act.

Source: [Google guidance on voice](https://developers.google.com/style/voice).
